# HealthTech visual constitution

**Direction: Scientific Editorial Abstraction.** Authoring revision: `scientific-1` (23 October 2026). The stored generation identity remains `healthtech-1.0`. [Editorial policy](../EDITORIAL.md) controls evidence and medical claims; this constitution controls art direction; [the illustration system](ILLUSTRATION_SYSTEM_V1.md) controls production and recovery. Use the actual HealthTech renderer and CSS for placement, never a source publication's mockup.

## One scientific idea, from verified context

Every newly prepared public article from 22 October 2026 needs accepted story-specific artwork. Missing or failed artwork withholds that story; a ledger-only edition needs none. Keep historical reviews as evidence of their original process, not approvals under this direction.

Start from two or three supported context sentences and one editorial question. Identify which visible elements are source-supported structures, which are deliberate simplifications, and which are metaphor. State what the image must not imply. The image must illuminate the article's scientific question or medical process, rather than merely announce “healthcare + AI”.

Prefer recognisable medical and biological forms: simplified cell membranes and nuclei, tissue-like layers, anatomically informed contours, specimen sections, microscopy-inspired textures or a medically relevant observation/process. Select them because the story supports them. A decorative cell, DNA helix or organ added to unrelated reporting fails specificity. Regulatory and documentation stories may use a medical process or boundary; do not force cellular imagery into them.

Create commissioned science illustration with deliberate composition, simple organic forms, tactile texture, subtle depth, intentional negative space and one memorable focal relationship. Fine lines can support larger forms, but must not carry the whole composition. Avoid generic geometric posters, architectural metaphors without a scientific connection, diagram-only flowcharts, glossy medical stock rendering and molecular wallpaper. Abstraction should simplify the science, not erase its identity.

## Quiet Laboratory palette and Edition Front

| Role                        | Colour                                     |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Primary field               | White `#FFFFFF`                            |
| Structure and strong detail | Ink `#424242`, teals `#51796E` / `#344F51` |
| Layers, quiet depth         | Pale sage `#E8EFEC`, mist `#D1E0E2`        |
| Limited focal accents       | Rose `#D4A298`, caramel `#BC8A5F`          |

Keep white dominant, dark structure legible and accents sparing. Avoid cream washes, Daily AI Pulse's warm-paper/terracotta treatment, excessive gradients, neon or synthetic glow. Essential meaning must also be conveyed by shape, position and text outside the image, not colour alone. Preserve Edition Front typography, hierarchy and layout.

Generate standalone, wordless artwork. Astro supplies headlines, metadata, labels, captions and disclosure. Do not bake a publication layout into the pixels.

## Scientific and medical hard gate

Reject a violation before aesthetic review:

- Unsupported anatomy, diagnostic features, molecular interactions, gene-regulatory edges or mechanisms presented as factual. Simplified biological forms are allowed when clearly illustrative; details that carry factual meaning need primary-source verification.
- Generated medical scans, histology presented as a real specimen, documentary-looking experiments, identifiable patients/researchers/institutions, invented study hardware, clinical success or harm, treatment recommendations or regulatory endorsement.
- Invented data, charts, numbers, measured proportions, density, zone ratios or performance encoded through size, arrows, colours or symbols. Do not imply “healthy versus diseased” or “before versus cured” through palette.
- Words, letters, numbers, labels, logos, interfaces, screenshots, page furniture, watermarks, borders or frames.
- Glowing brains, humanoid robots, decorative floating molecules, stock AI icons, glossy 3D objects or sensational illness imagery.

An editorial metaphor is not an explanatory scientific diagram. Factual diagrams use verified source data, reproducible code and scientific review; use correctly credited source material only after rights and the current generated-only content contract have been addressed. Never label sourced evidence as generated. This change does not expand the content schema to a new media mode.

For the B-cell pilot, a stylised cellular field and separate inferred traces can evoke inference from cellular snapshots. They must not reproduce an actual microscopy image, imply an established pathway, encode rescue magnitudes or suggest human therapeutic benefit.

## Concepts, candidates and acceptance

For routine production, write three materially different concepts, choose one with reasons and start with one generated candidate. The issue #52 direction-setting pilot generates three genuinely different compositions for **one** article. Palette swaps do not count. Record actual outputs and counts; text concepts are not image candidates. Permit at most one revised batch per invocation; checkpoint unresolved failure instead of looping.

Inspect actual pixels beside headline, description, lead and disclosure. Reject unsupported medical inference first. Then record an observed pass/fail reason for:

1. **Specificity:** the medical/biological anchor and focal relationship fit this article's actual question; a palette match alone fails.
2. **Meaning:** distinguish scientific structure, simplification and metaphor; avoid an invented mechanism or an interchangeable science motif.
3. **Clarity and craft:** clean forms and texture, no accidental symbols or artifacts; focal relationship survives a 160-pixel thumbnail, desktop 16:9 article crop and 3:2 listing/mobile crops using real placements.
4. **Variety:** compare up to five recent approved HealthTech assets and current candidates. Record actual IDs or unavailable comparisons. Do not call Daily artwork an approved HealthTech reference.

Review actual page contexts, alt text and visible generated-illustration disclosure on desktop and mobile. Alt describes visible content; captions distinguish representation from study evidence. Attractive artwork and green CI cannot override a failed medical or editorial gate. Record model self-review as model review, not independent biological validation.
